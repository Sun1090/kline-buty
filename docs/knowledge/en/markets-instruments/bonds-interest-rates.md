# Bonds and Interest Rates: The Pricing Anchor of Assets

> Stocks, forex, and crypto all have their speculators, but **what truly determines "how much money is worth" are bonds and interest rates**. When the Fed hikes, global stocks tremble, gold falls, crypto crashes — the starting point of the transmission chain is always the bond market.
>
> This article clarifies three things: **how bonds are priced, what the **<mark>yield</mark>** curve is saying, and how interest rates rule all assets**. This is not proprietary knowledge for "bond people" — it is foundational common sense every trader should own.

---

## 1. What Is a Bond

A bond is essentially an **IOU**: the issuer (government/corporation) borrows from you and promises to repay principal plus interest at maturity.

| Element | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Face value** | Principal repaid at maturity | 100 yuan |
| **Coupon rate** | Interest paid each year as a percentage | 3% → 3 yuan per year |
| **Maturity** | Repayment date | 10-year → repay 100 yuan in 10 years |
| **Price** | What you pay in the secondary market | May be above or below face value |
| **Yield to maturity (YTM)** | The true annualized return from buying at the current price and holding to maturity | Backed out from the market price |

**One sentence: the coupon rate is "the rate written in the contract"; YTM is "the actual rate you get by buying now".** The two differ because the price moves.

---

## 2. Price and Yield: The Inverse Relationship (with Numeric Examples)

::: tip 💡 The first iron law of the bond market
**Bond prices and yields move inversely**: rates rise → old bonds cut price → after the cut, the yield on the cheaper entry rises until it matches new bonds.
:::

Why? Because the coupon is fixed. When market rates rise, newly issued bonds pay higher coupons; old bonds become unwanted and can only sell at a **discount**; once discounted, the yield computed at the lower price rises until it aligns with new-bond yields.

### Numeric example

- A treasury with face value 100 yuan, 3% coupon, 10 years remaining; comparable new bonds in the market yield 4%
- The old bond pays only 3 yuan a year, so its appeal drops → price falls to about **91.8 yuan**
- You buy at 91.8 and hold to maturity: 3 yuan × 10 years + 100 yuan back at maturity → actual annualized ≈ **4%**
- Conversely, if market rates fall to 2%, this 3% bond becomes sought after → price rises to about **109 yuan**

**Duration** measures this sensitivity: the longer the duration, the more sensitive the price is to rate changes. A 30-year treasury is far more sensitive to hikes than a 2-year — one reason long US Treasuries crashed in price during the Fed's aggressive 2022 tightening.

> The prices above come from the bond pricing formula (discounted cash flow); this example uses teaching-basis approximations — **exact prices follow live quotes**.

## 3. Bond Categories: From Treasuries to NCDs

| Type | Issuer | Risk | Return | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Treasury (gilt)** | Central government | Near zero (in domestic currency) | Low | The risk-free-rate benchmark; domestically book-entry/savings treasuries |
| Local government bonds | Local governments | Very low | Slightly above treasuries | Backed by local fiscal credit |
| **Financial bonds** | Banks and other financial institutions | Low | Medium | Policy-bank bonds are a mainstay institutional allocation |
| **Corporate bonds** | Corporations | Depends on credit rating | Medium-high | Lower rating = higher yield (credit spread) |
| **Convertible bonds** | Listed companies | Hybrid equity-debt | Medium | Convertible into stock at a set price; floor below, open ceiling above |
| Subordinated/perpetual bonds | Banks/corporations | Higher | Higher | Low priority in repayment; "write-down" clause risk |
| **Negotiable certificates of deposit (NCDs)** | Banks | Low | Short-term pricing | Interbank money-market instrument; individuals cannot buy directly |

**Credit spread** is the core concept: for the same maturity, a corporate bond's yield minus the treasury yield is the market's "default compensation" to it. Spreads widen in bad times and narrow in good times.

## 4. The Risk-Free Rate: The Pricing Baseline of All Assets

The **risk-free rate** is usually represented by the treasury yield (typically the 10-year, on both domestic and foreign conventions). It is the "gravity" of the financial world:

```text
Fair asset value = Future cash flows / (1 + risk-free rate + risk premium) ^ years
```

The meaning is plain: **if money parked risk-free earns 4%, why take risk for an asset with only a 3% expected return?** Hence:

- Higher risk-free rate → higher "discount rate" for all assets → lower discounted value of future cash flows → **stocks, housing, and long bonds all fall**
- Lower risk-free rate → capital is forced to "search for yield" → risk assets, gold, and crypto rise

::: danger 💀 Rising rates are the strongest asset killer
**The higher the risk-free rate, the higher the "discount rate" on all assets and the lower the discounted value of future cash flows — stocks, housing, and long bonds all fall.** If money parked risk-free earns 4%, why take risk for 3% expected? When rates rise, risk assets take collective pressure.
:::

> In China, the 10-year treasury yield is commonly used as the domestic risk-free-rate reference (it has hovered around 2% since 2024, **per the latest quotes**).

## 5. The Yield Curve: The Economy's Thermometer

Plot the yields of bonds from **the same issuer (e.g., the US Treasury) across maturities** — that line is the yield curve.

| Shape | Long end vs. short end | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Normal/steep (upward)** | Short low, long high | Normal expansion; capital willingly bets on long-term returns |
| **Flat** | Long ≈ short | Sensitive to hiking cycles; growth momentum in question |
| **Inverted (downward)** | **Short above long** | Market expects the economy to worsen and the central bank to cut — **one of history's most reliable recession warnings** |

### Why does inversion = recession signal?

Normally, borrowing for 10 years should cost more than 1 year (term compensation). **Inversion means the market would rather lock in long than borrow short** — typically late in an aggressive hiking cycle: the short end is pushed up by the policy rate while the market strongly bets on future cuts (recession). Historically, **after the US 2Y-10Y spread turns negative, a recession has usually followed within 12–24 months** (a statistical pattern, not a certainty).

> Note: **inversion is a "signal", not a "trigger"** — after inversion appears, the economy often holds on for another six months or more before recession. Wait for confirmation when trading it; don't front-run.

::: warning ⚠️ Inversion is a signal, not a trigger
**Inversion is a "signal", not a "trigger"** — after inversion appears, the economy often holds on for another six months or more before recession. That a recession follows within 12–24 months of the US 2Y-10Y spread turning negative is a statistical pattern, not a certainty — don't front-run a position on inversion.
:::

## 6. The US 10-Year: The Global Asset-Pricing Anchor

Why does the whole world watch the **US 10-year Treasury yield (US 10Y)**? Because the dollar is the global reserve currency and US Treasuries are the world's safest and most **<mark>liquid</mark>** assets — they are **the global risk-free-rate benchmark**, the "origin of coordinates" for pricing all dollar assets.

### Transmission chain

```text
US 10Y yield moves
    ├─→ Gold: yield up → opportunity cost of holding gold up → gold down (clearest negative correlation)
    ├─→ US stocks: risk-free rate up → equity discount rate up → high-valuation growth pressured
    │        (tech most sensitive, future cash flows concentrated far out)
    ├─→ Global bonds: every country's yields follow (spread transmission)
    ├─→ Emerging markets: capital flows back to dollar assets; stocks, bonds, FX hit together
    └─→ Crypto: risk-asset sympathy; pressured when liquidity tightens
```

### Practical significance (with 2022–2023 as reference)

- In 2022 the Fed hiked aggressively and the US 10Y soared from 1.5% to 4%+: **US stocks fell, gold fell, BTC dropped from 60k+ to near 15k** — indiscriminate selling across all markets, a textbook case of rates dominating everything
- From 2024, cut expectations warmed and the US 10Y pulled back: gold hit all-time highs, BTC returned to 60k+, US stocks set repeated highs — **an easing liquidity cycle opened, risk assets led**

> Linkage patterns are "statistical correlations" with lags and exceptions. **Watching the marginal change in the US 10Y (rising/falling direction) is more useful than the absolute level.**

### Domestic rates and US Treasuries: the China-US spread

Domestic assets (A-shares, CNY bonds) are influenced by both **domestic rates** and **US Treasury yields**:

| Scenario | Impact |
|---|---|
| China-US spread (CNY − USD) narrows/inverts | Depreciation pressure on the renminbi; foreign outflows from A-shares and domestic bonds |
| China-US spread widens | Renminbi appreciation, foreign inflows; positive for domestic assets |
| US yields spike independently | Even with domestic cuts, A-shares and the renminbi can still be "drained" by the offshore market |

> The China-US treasury spread is a key gauge of cross-border capital flows (in recent years it has hovered around 0 or even inverted, **per the latest data**). Don't watch only domestic policy for A-shares — **the US 10Y is the anchor of global liquidity.**

## 7. The Domestic Bond Market and Retail Plays

### Interbank vs. exchange

| Market | Participants | Characteristics |
|---|---|---|
| **Interbank market** | Banks, insurers, funds and other institutions | 90%+ of bond turnover; individuals cannot participate directly |
| **Exchange market** | Institutions + qualified retail | Treasuries, convertibles, corporate bonds tradable |

### Treasury reverse repo: the retail "idle cash" tool

**Treasury reverse repo** is the only money-market instrument retail investors can access directly: you lend money to institutions that need funds, they pledge **treasuries as collateral**, and repay principal plus interest at maturity — in essence "short-term lending secured by treasuries", virtually risk-free.

- How to: securities account → sell/reverse repo (enter a code such as GC001/204001; Shanghai from 100k yuan, Shenzhen from 1,000 yuan, **per the latest rules**)
- Feature: **rates spike at month-end, quarter-end, year-end, and before long holidays** (institutions are short of cash); historically 5%-10% or higher annualized (pre-holiday) versus a usual 1%-2%
- Interest accrues by actual days of funds occupied; funds usable T+1

### Pure bond funds

- Funds that invest only in bonds — low volatility, low return (historically about 2%-5% annualized, **per the latest performance**)
- Suit: money unsatisfied with money-fund yields but unwilling to bear equity swings
- Note: **bond funds can lose money too** — when rates spike or credit defaults hit, NAV also **<mark>draws down</mark>** (the broad November 2022 pullback in domestic bond funds was driven by rising rates)

## 8. Hiking and Cutting Cycles: Transmission Paths by Asset

| Phase | Central-bank action | Asset performance (historical experience, not guaranteed) |
|---|---|---|
| **Cutting cycle** | Rate cuts, balance-sheet expansion | Bond bull (rates fall, prices rise), stock rebound, gold stronger, property benefits, dollar softer, EM benefits |
| **Hiking cycle** | Rate hikes, balance-sheet contraction | Bonds pressured, stocks (especially growth) correct, gold pressured, dollar stronger, high rates suppress property and credit |
| **Late hiking** | After the final hike | Stocks often see a "final drop then reversal"; gold rebounds first; bonds front-run cut expectations |
| **Late cutting** | Recovery after cuts | Yield curve re-steepens; stocks enter their main advancing leg |

### Key points

- Markets trade **expectations**, not facts: **the expectation phase of "the central bank will hike" does far more damage than hike day itself** (when it lands, it is "bad news fully priced")

::: danger 💀 Expected hikes hurt more than the hike itself
**The expectation phase of "the central bank will hike" does far more damage than the hike's landing day** — because markets trade expectations, not facts. During the expectation phase, asset prices keep getting discounted and **<mark>positions</mark>** get passively trimmed; once the hike lands, it is often "bad news fully priced" and assets may even rebound. Making decisions from facts guarantees you are a beat late.
:::

- Stocks are the "leading indicator", bonds the "coincident indicator", gold "the mirror of expectations" — grasp this timing gap and you understand why in September 2024, before the Fed had even cut, gold and US stocks had already rallied a round

> The above is a teaching summary of historical statistical patterns and **does not constitute a forecast for any cycle phase**.

## 9. How Ordinary Investors Access Bonds

| Route | Threshold | Suits | Characteristics |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Savings treasuries** | From 100 yuan (per latest rules) | Conservative | Certificate/electronic; hold to maturity for principal + interest; not transferable |
| **Exchange treasuries** | ~1,000 yuan face value | Those with a securities account | Real-time trading; price moves with rates |
| **Treasury reverse repo** | Shenzhen from 1,000 yuan | Idle-cash holders | Nearly risk-free; big pre-holiday yield pops |
| **Pure/short bond funds** | From 10 yuan | Steady type | Low threshold, good liquidity, small swings |
| **Convertible bonds** | ~100 yuan face value per bond | Advanced | Debt floor below, equity upside above; must learn the terms |
| **Bond ETFs** | On-exchange | Liquidity seekers | E.g., treasury ETFs, corporate bond ETFs (codes per latest) |

**Suggested beginner path**: treasury reverse repo (learn the mechanics) → savings treasuries (lock the risk-free return) → pure bond funds (delegate to professionals) → convertibles (only after advancing).

## Risk Warning

::: warning ⚠️ Risk Warning
"Risk-free" is relative: credit risk (default), interest-rate risk (price swings), and inflation risk (purchasing-power erosion) all exist for bonds. **Even treasury reverse repo carries counterparty default and liquidity risk (however tiny).** Signals such as curve inversion are statistical patterns and must not be the sole basis for a trade. Rates, yields, and fees in this article are teaching-basis reference values — **always defer to the latest quotes and regulations**. This article does not constitute investment advice.
:::
