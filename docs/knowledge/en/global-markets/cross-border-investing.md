# 05 · Cross-Border Investing in Practice (Pragmatic Guide)

> The first four articles answered "what is out there"; this one answers the most practical question: "how do I participate compliantly". This article teaches no side doors around regulation — it lays out goal setting, legal paths for moving funds offshore, how to choose an offshore broker, tax filing obligations, and a risk checklist — the core conclusion in one sentence: **<mark>for most people QDII + Stock Connect are enough; only a minority need to consider a regulated offshore broker, provided every step of moving funds offshore is compliant.</mark>**

---

## 1. Goal Setting: Be Clear About "Why Go Offshore" First

### 1.1 Three Commonly Cited Reasons (Objective Analysis)

| Reason | Logic | Objective assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Currency diversification | All-RMB assets carry no **<mark>hedge</mark>** against exchange-rate swings; holding some foreign-currency assets diversifies single-currency risk | Valid, but the price is "offshore assets shrink when the RMB appreciates" |
| Scarce instruments | Offshore assets unavailable in A-shares: global tech giants, rare ETFs, US Treasuries, REITs, etc. | Valid, but most needs can already be covered by QDII funds |
| Tax planning | Some overseas regimes may differ on taxes (capital gains, dividend withholding, etc.) | **<mark>The most misunderstood item</mark>**: Chinese tax residents' worldwide-income filing obligation does not depend on where assets sit (see Section 4); "tax avoidance" is not a legitimate reason to go offshore |

### 1.2 The "Three Necessity Questions" Before Going Offshore

> **Ask yourself three questions before acting (important):**
> 1. Have you achieved equity/bond ratios, DCA, and rebalancing discipline in your domestic portfolio? — For most people the allocation gap exists at home; going offshore is not the cure.
> 2. Will this money be untouched for 5+ years? — Cross-border money movement is costly and slow, unsuitable for short-term churn.
> 3. Can you accept "gaining offshore, losing on FX, and netting nothing after converting back to RMB"? — Yen depreciation eating up Japanese stock gains is a real, repeatedly seen case in recent years (historical market action).

**Common knowledge**: going offshore means "trading extra cost for diversification", not a shortcut to "earn more"; without a genuine cross-border need (study abroad, work, immigration, medical care) or sufficient assets, most people do not need an offshore account at all.

::: tip The First Principle of Going Offshore
**Going offshore means "trading extra cost for diversification", not a shortcut to "earn more".** Without genuine cross-border needs or sufficient assets, most people do not need an offshore account — the allocation gap exists at home; going offshore is not the cure.

## 2. Compliant Paths for Moving Funds Offshore (Subject to the latest regulations)

### 2.1 Personal FX Quota: The Truth About USD 50,000

- **Common knowledge**: personal FX settlement follows a **<mark>USD 50,000 annual facilitation quota per person</mark>** (historical policy framing, subject to the current rules of the State Administration of Foreign Exchange); within the quota it is handled with an ID card.
- **Quota ≠ freedom of use**: the facilitation quota mainly covers current-account items (tourism, study abroad, medical care, family visits — genuine needs); **<mark>capital-account</mark>** items (buying property offshore, offshore securities investment, etc.) cannot be freely handled within this quota and require specially approved channels.
- When purchasing FX you must fill in the individual FX purchase form and truthfully declare the purpose; false declarations have consequences.
- **Two high-risk practices (risk warning, not operational guidance)**:
  - "**<mark>Ant-moving</mark>** split transactions": multiple people splitting FX settlement into transfers to the same offshore account → may be placed on a watchlist and lose the facilitation quota for the current and future years (Subject to the latest regulations).
  - "**<mark>Underground banks</mark>**": convert to RMB onshore, lend offshore → illegal FX trading / money-laundering territory; if funds are frozen or the operator disappears there is no recourse.

### 2.2 Comparison of Four Compliant Paths

| Path | How funds go offshore | Barrier | Coverage | Main restrictions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stock Connect (Shanghai/Shenzhen–Hong Kong) | **No FX conversion needed**, settled directly in RMB (official connect mechanism) | RMB 500k in securities account assets (historical threshold, subject to the latest exchange rules) | Hong Kong stocks within the Stock Connect eligible universe | Universe excludes US/Europe/Japan markets; FX shows up over the holding period |
| QDII funds | Fund company converts FX centrally; individuals need not convert themselves | None; from ~RMB 1,000 | Overseas funds covering US/Japan/Europe/emerging markets | On-exchange premium (commonly 5%-20%), purchase limits, slow redemptions (T+7 common) |
| Offshore bank/broker account | Must complete compliant offshore transfer yourself (see 2.3) | Account-opening scrutiny tightening (deposit requirements, source-of-funds proof common) | Global markets + global products | Offshore compliance is the core difficulty; channels may change with policy |
| Compliant FX channels (study abroad/medical etc.) | Handled with documents proving genuine purpose | Proof documents required | Current-account genuine needs only | Cannot be used for securities investment |

::: warning ⚠️ Channels Can Change Anytime
**Special reminder**: whichever path you choose, every step of "FX conversion → cross-border transfer → funding in" must survive a compliance audit — **channels can change anytime (historically tightened repeatedly); always defer to current regulations and the latest replies from banks/exchanges.**
:::

### 2.3 Offshore Bank/Broker Accounts: Compliance Conditions for Remote Account Opening (common knowledge)

- **Witnessed account opening**: some domestic banks offer remote witnessed opening of Hong Kong accounts (offered by some banks, scrutiny tightening); or open in person in Hong Kong/offshore — the most traditional compliant path.
- **Source-of-funds proof**: opening offshore accounts and funding them generally requires proof of source and purpose of funds (salary statements, tax payment records, etc.).
- **Remote account opening today**: some international brokers support remote online account opening (e.g., Interactive Brokers), but funding still requires compliantly transferred offshore funds; intermediary services promising "no documents needed, instant open, instant funding" almost certainly involve non-compliant channels.

## 2.5 Stock Connect Details and QDII Selection Points

### Stock Connect (if you meet the threshold)

| Dimension | Common knowledge |
|---|---|
| Settlement currency | Settled directly in RMB (no FX conversion needed) — its biggest compliance advantage |
| Eligible universe | Hong Kong stocks on the Stock Connect list (Hang Seng Composite LargeCap/MidCap etc., subject to the latest exchange lists) |
| Trading rules | HK stocks trade T+0, settle T+2; no daily price limits; odd-lot (less than one board lot) handling differs from A-shares |
| Exchange rate | RMB/HKD conversion done by the settlement bank at T+2 settlement; cost embedded in the trade |
| Daily quota | Each leg of Stock Connect has a daily quota cap (subject to current exchange announcements); in extreme markets you may find "no quota left to buy" |

### QDII Fund Selection Points

| Dimension | Common knowledge |
|---|---|
| Tracking target | Check whether it tracks Nikkei 225 or TOPIX, S&P 500 or Nasdaq 100 — same-named funds may track completely different indices |
| On-exchange vs OTC | On-exchange (ETF units) trades in real time but carries a premium; OTC (subscription) transacts at NAV but with purchase limits and slow redemptions |
| Fees | QDII management fees are generally higher than ordinary domestic funds (common knowledge); subscription/redemption and custody fees must be counted into costs |
| Tracking error | Due to quotas, FX conversion, and cash **<mark>positioning</mark>**, QDII tracking error is generally larger than domestic ETFs — look at "deviation", not just the name |
| Size and **<mark>liquidity</mark>** | Prefer products above RMB 500m in size with active on-exchange turnover; avoid liquidation risk of tiny funds |

::: warning ⚠️ QDII Premium Self-Check
**Common knowledge**: the QDII premium can be checked in market software by comparing the on-exchange price with "IOPV (indicative optimized portfolio value)" — premium rate = (on-exchange price − IOPV) / IOPV. Above 5% be alert; above 10% buying is basically a guaranteed loss (has occurred repeatedly historically).
:::

## 3. Choosing an Offshore Broker and Opening an Account

### 3.1 Mainstream Brokers and Licenses (common knowledge)

| Broker | Main licenses (common knowledge) | Traits |
|---|---|---|
| Interactive Brokers (IBKR) | FINRA/SEC registered in the US, member of multiple exchanges | Full global coverage, low fees, professional tools; suits advanced investors |
| Futu/Tiger etc. | Hong Kong SFC license + US broker-dealer licenses (subject to each firm's current license status) | Friendly Chinese interface, strong HK IPO subscription features; suits HK/US retail investors |
| Charles Schwab, Vanguard etc. traditional brokers | US-registered broker-dealers | Long-established service, full product range; account opening usually requires US or international address documents |

::: info 📖 How to Verify a Broker's License
**License common knowledge**: US brokers must register with FINRA (Financial Industry Regulatory Authority)/SEC (US Securities and Exchange Commission); Hong Kong brokers must hold an SFC (Securities and Futures Commission) license. **Checking license status is lesson one of choosing a broker** — any institution's registration record can be looked up online (FINRA BrokerCheck, Hong Kong SFC public register, etc.).
:::

### 3.2 Common Knowledge on Fund Safety

- US brokers are covered by **<mark>SIPC</mark>** (Securities Investor Protection Corporation) (common knowledge: roughly USD 500k cap per customer); Hong Kong brokers by the Hong Kong Investor Compensation Fund (subject to the current compensation cap) — **protection has caps and mainly covers broker bankruptcy/misappropriation, not investment losses**.
- **Custody common knowledge**: legitimate brokers segregate client assets from the firm's proprietary assets; anyone asking you to wire funds directly to a "personal account" instead of an institutional account can basically be judged a scam.
- Withdrawal test: after opening, test deposits and withdrawals once with a small amount — **only a smooth deposit-withdrawal path counts as a working path**.

### 3.3 Fee Comparison (common knowledge)

| Item | US brokers like IBKR (common knowledge) | Futu/Tiger etc. (common knowledge) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| US stock commission | Per-share/per-trade, mostly low commissions | Per-trade, slightly higher | Subject to each firm's current fee schedule |
| FX cost | Low internal conversion **<mark>spread</mark>** | Some conversion **<mark>basis points</mark>** | Conversion cost is often overlooked — one of the biggest actual fees |
| Platform/custody fees | Some accounts have monthly activity fees | Mostly none | Watch monthly fees for small low-frequency accounts |
| Deposit/withdrawal fees | Wire fee + intermediary bank fee | Same as left | Hundreds of RMB per transfer (subject to each firm) |

## 3.5 Offshore Broker Account Opening and Funding Steps (common knowledge)

1. **Choose a broker**: verify licenses on official sites (FINRA BrokerCheck / Hong Kong SFC public register); read deposit/withdrawal policies and fee schedules.
2. **Open online**: submit identity proof (ID card/passport), address proof (required by some brokers), tax residency declaration (W-8BEN form etc., used to claim treaty benefits).
3. **Source-of-funds review**: some brokers require proof of source of funds (salary flow, asset proof) — accounts that cannot explain sources may be rejected or restricted.
4. **Compliant funding**: wire funds that went offshore via a compliant path to the broker account; **any "top-up service" or "third-party transfer" violates broker compliance requirements**.
5. **Withdrawal test**: first test the return path "broker → offshore bank → onshore" with a small amount.
6. **Ongoing attention**: update tax residency information yearly; follow the broker's regulator notices and CRS reporting reminders.

> **Common knowledge**: opening the account itself has no barrier; the real barrier is "compliant funds + compliant repatriation". Many people get stuck at step one — not because they cannot open an account, but because they **cannot explain the source of funds**. Honesty, traceability, and explainability are the only moat of a cross-border account.

::: danger The Real Barrier of a Cross-Border Account
**Opening the account has no barrier; the real barrier is "compliant funds + compliant repatriation" — many get stuck at step one, unable to explain their source of funds rather than unable to open.** Honesty, traceability, and explainability are the only moat; any "top-up service" or "third-party transfer" violates broker compliance requirements.
:::

## 4. Taxes and Filing (educational common knowledge)

### 4.1 CRS: The Information Exchange Mechanism

- **<mark>CRS (Common Reporting Standard)</mark>**: OECD-led multilateral tax information exchange mechanism; China has participated since 2017 (historical framing).
- Mechanism: financial institutions in participating jurisdictions identify account holders' **tax residency**, and batch-exchange balances, interest, dividends and other information of non-resident accounts back to their jurisdiction of tax residence.
- **What it means for you**: financial account information held by Chinese tax residents in CRS participating countries/regions (Hong Kong, Singapore, Switzerland, the US (the US did not join CRS; it runs FATCA separately), etc.) is periodically exchanged back to Chinese tax authorities.
- **Common knowledge**: CRS is not "instant tax auditing" but "information transparency infrastructure" — the era of "hiding money offshore where nobody knows" is over.

### 4.2 Chinese Tax Residents and Filing Obligations

- **<mark>Chinese tax resident concept</mark>**: domiciled in China, or without domicile but residing in China for 183 days or more in a tax year (subject to the latest tax law).
- Chinese tax residents should declare and pay individual income tax on **<mark>worldwide income</mark>**: overseas salary, dividends, interest, capital gains, rents, etc. are all taxable.
- Tax already paid overseas may be credited according to law (foreign tax credit system, subject to the latest tax law and treaties).
- **Status note**: under China's current tax law, individual offshore securities investment gains (capital gains) **lack clear implementing rules** — a gray area of "filing obligation exists, enforcement unclear" (descriptive common knowledge). But this does not mean "no need to file": truthful declaration is the only safe option; consult a professional tax advisor.

## 4.5 Tax Common Knowledge on Offshore Investment Income (subject to the latest tax law)

| Investment type | Typical tax treatment (common knowledge) |
|---|---|
| US stock dividends | The US levies dividend withholding tax on Chinese residents (common-knowledge distinction between treaty rates and the 30% standard rate, subject to current IRS rules); withheld automatically by the broker |
| HK stock dividends | Hong Kong levies no dividend tax (common knowledge); mainland individuals pay 20% dividend tax via Stock Connect (historical policy framing, subject to the latest rules) |
| Offshore capital gains | Chinese tax residents owe worldwide-income filing obligations, but collection rules for offshore securities capital gains currently lack clear implementation provisions (gray area; subject to current tax authority practice) |
| Offshore interest | Offshore deposit/bond interest is taxable income and must be self-declared |
| Foreign tax credit | Income tax already paid offshore may credit domestic tax payable within limits (subject to the latest tax law and treaties) |

::: warning ⚠️ The Gray Zone of Tax Filing
**Common knowledge**: dividend withholding is deducted at the broker end and needs no self-handling; capital gains and interest fall in the zone of "filing obligation exists, collection practice unclear" — **do not conceal proactively, and do not improvise without professional advice**; truthful declaration plus consulting a tax advisor is the only safe option.
:::

## 4.6 Money Coming Back: How Funds Return (common knowledge)

- **Repatriation is the legal direction**: remitting offshore income back into RMB is normal capital flow (Subject to the latest regulations), opposite in direction to the strict controls on moving money out.
- Process common knowledge: offshore broker/bank → remit to domestic account → settle to RMB; large settlements require declaring source of funds (salary, investment income, etc.).
- **Common misconception**: assuming "hard to leave, easy to come back" — in fact large inflows also trigger anti-money-laundering checks by banks and FX regulators; money with unexplainable origins may be refused.
- **Common knowledge**: when planning outbound funds, plan the return path too — **only a path that works both ways is a complete path**.

## 5. Cross-Border Investing Risk Checklist

| Risk | Concrete manifestation (common knowledge) | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Currency risk | Offshore gains eaten by FX (yen depreciation case); two-way spreads on conversion | Evaluate in RMB terms; no high-frequency in-and-out |
| Legal compliance risk | Legal consequences of ant-moving splits, underground banks, false declarations | Official channels only; keep evidence at every step |
| Channel closure risk | Broker funding channels and banks' cross-border services may change anytime | Test withdrawal paths in advance; diversify channels |
| **Fraud risk (fake brokers)** | Fake famous-broker websites/Apps, stock-tip groups plus "helped account opening and funding", requests to transfer to personal accounts | Verify licenses via official channels; never pay into personal accounts |
| Information disadvantage | Asymmetry in language, filings, regulatory information | Prefer standardized products like index ETFs; stay away from complex products |

::: danger ⚠️ Three Principles for Spotting Scams
**Three principles for spotting scams (important)**: ① check licenses (FINRA/SFC official websites); ② funds go only into institutional custodial corporate accounts; ③ anything claiming "internal channels", "guaranteed high returns", or asking for an upfront **<mark>margin deposit</mark>** is a scam.
:::

## 6. Summary: The Best Solution for Ordinary People

| Audience | Best solution (common-knowledge suggestion, not investment advice) |
|---|---|
| Most people (just want diversification) | **QDII funds primarily**: no threshold, clean compliance, just learn to read on-exchange premiums; HK stocks via Stock Connect (if you meet the RMB 500k threshold) |
| Need HK stocks but below threshold | QDII Hong Kong stock funds; or open Stock Connect after qualifying |
| A few people (genuine cross-border needs + compliant funds) | Regulated offshore broker (verifiable license) + compliantly moved funds + understanding CRS and filing obligations |
| Not recommended | Underground banks, ant-moving splits, unlicensed intermediaries, fake brokers — each carries expected cost above expected benefit |

**Three-sentence summary**:

1. Think through **necessity** before acting: without genuine need, staying home is the optimal solution.
2. **Compliance** is the precondition for everything: follow current regulations at every step; if a channel changes, you bear the consequences.
3. Count **fees and FX** before investing: fund premiums, conversion spreads, and wire fees together may be the invisible bulk of actual returns.
