# 24 · Career Development

> The previous 23 chapters answered "**how to understand markets and how to trade**"; this chapter answers the final question: **after learning all this, how do you turn it into a career (quant job hunting), a business (indie development / content creation), and a lifestyle (professional trading)**.
>
> This chapter does not sell the myth of "financial freedom", nor does it manufacture "career-switch anxiety". It does one thing only: lay out the real options, entry barriers, costs, and probabilities on the path from "trading/finance skills → career and business", so you can weigh them against your own situation (personality, capital, family, time) before choosing.
>
> **Disclaimer**: All content on this site is for learning and research only and does not constitute investment advice. All salary figures, entry barriers, and success rates mentioned are public common knowledge and rough ranges; actual market conditions prevail. Markets are risky; invest with caution.

---

## Where This Chapter Fits: From "Being Able to Trade" to "Living Off Trading"

```text
Chapters 1-23 done → you can trade, understand markets, write strategies, read financial statements
     ↓
This chapter answers: "How do these skills become income?"
     ↓
Three routes · Three lifestyles
```

- **Career** (articles 01, 02): sell your skills to institutions — quant funds / prop desks / brokers / foreign firms, for salary and bonus.
- **Business** (articles 03, 04): turn skills into products and content — indie tools, courses, communities, where income scales with influence.
- **Lifestyle** (article 05): neither employed nor a founder, living off your own trading system — freedom and risk in equal, enormous measure.

The three routes are not mutually exclusive: many people first work as a quant developer (career), create content on the side (business), then consider professional trading (lifestyle) once they've saved enough capital; others fail as full-time traders first and flow back into employment. **This chapter suggests you treat them as a "portfolio of options", not a single-choice question.**

## Career Route Map

| Your profile | Recommended route | Document | Key barrier |
|---|---|---|---|
| STEM / strong coding, good at math | Quant (dev/research) → institutions | [01](quant-career-path.md) | Algorithms + probability/statistics + verifiable projects |
| Finance background, logical, stress-tolerant | Sell-side/buy-side research → prop trading | [02](trading-careers.md) | Degree/internship resume + research depth |
| Tech + product sense | Indie trading tools | [03](indie-startups.md) | Product ability + compliance red lines + uncertain long-term income |
| Good communicator, loves sharing | Financial content creation | [04](content-creation.md) | Compliance boundaries + consistent output + differentiation |
| Sufficient capital, stable psychology | Full-time trading | [05](professional-trader-path.md) | 2-year live track record + 3-year emergency fund + family consensus |
| None of the above | Keep your day job, transition part-time | [05](professional-trader-path.md) | See "Part-Time Transition Plans" |

> Rule of thumb: **technical → quant/development; trading-oriented → prop/professional trading; content-oriented → creation.** But rank by risk first: career (you lose time) < business (you lose time + small money) < professional trading (you lose principal + time + psychology). The further down the list, the more you should have proven yourself on earlier routes first.

## Content Conventions

- All salary figures, barriers, and success rates are **public common knowledge and rough ranges**; they change with market cycles and are marked "actual market conditions prevail".
- Institution names are industry common-knowledge examples only, not recommendations; no internal data or internal recruiting information is involved.
- Compliance-related content follows current regulations (e.g., the Interim Measures for the Administration of Securities and Futures Investment Consulting); consult professionals before conducting real business.
- Every article contains a "⚠️ Risk Warning": the risk in career choice lies not in "choosing wrong" but in "insufficient information + going all-in".

---

> **⚠️ Risk Warning**
>
> The biggest risk in turning trading/finance skills into a career is not the skill itself, but **making career decisions with a gambler's mindset**: quitting your job because you see someone trading full-time, jumping into courses because you see someone making money from them, rage-quitting into quant because the salaries look high. Each article in this series gives you "checklists" and "failure-rate realities" — treat them as benchmarks to compare against your current work/life, not as a source of courage to act. Every career path carries survivorship bias; think through "can I survive the worst outcome" before considering "how attractive is the best outcome".
